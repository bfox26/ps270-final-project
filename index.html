Introduction

The Woman’s National Basketball Association (WNBA) was founded in 1997 and originally comprised eight teams. In 2025 four teams of the original eight were still in the league. In 2025 the WNBA included 13 teams in the league. More support and spotlight has been on individual players and the league as a whole. It is important to note that in the data set the Utah Starzz are deemed an original team that are still in the league although they disbanded in 2002. This is because the Utah Starzz became the San Antonio Stars with the same players in 2003. The San Antonio Stars then became the Las Vegas Aces in 2018 with the same players from the San Antonio Stars 2017 season.

Does a team being a member of the WNBA since its founding increase away game attendance? Or do the newest four WNBA teams in 2025 get higher attendance at away games? I hypothesize that the inaugural teams` away game attendance have loyal fans but not as many compared to newer teams. This is because newer teams gain traction because of their players’ skills. While inaugural teams fans are loyal and likely have supported the team since the beginning. Therefore, the amount of support is less likely to increase without the excitement of a new team or a new player. This question is important because the WNBA is under research. Since it is a relatively new league there are few data sets and historical information available about the league. Unlike the NBA which has been around for 77 years and is well known by the general public. Given that this research question is focused on an all women league, sexism plays a role. This fact makes this research question more interesting and important to analyze.

The data source is called “All Game Attendance” and it measures the date of WNBA games, the home and away teams playing, attendance, game type, arena, and game location. The dependent variable of my analysis is attendance and my independent variable is the type of Away Team. The four inaugural teams that are still in the WNBA in 2025 and the four newest WNBA teams in 2025. The dependent variable, attendance, is measured quantitatively. This is because the researcher was able to find the number of fan attendance and it allows you to compute the mean and standard deviation of the variable. The Away team is measured by observation. In the data set the researcher only states which team is playing as the “Home Team” or the “Away Team.” The knowledge about which teams joined the league more recently or in 1997 comes from outside knowledge.

My research design is cross-sectional because I am analyzing away game attendance for four inaugural teams and four of the newest teams in the WNBA in 2025. Although some of these teams have been in the league since 1997, I am only examining their attendance data at a single point in time. The key independent variable is whether a team is an inaugural or newer team, which is categorical, while the dependent variable is away game attendance, which is continuous. Because all observations occur in 2025 and compare different teams at that moment, this study is cross-sectional.

To support my hypothesis the newer WNBA teams would have to have a higher average away game attendance than newer WNBA teams. I would also have to see that my regression showed a positive and statistically significant number for the independent variable. This would represent that newer teams have a higher and different away game attendance than inaugural WNBA teams. To reject my hypothesis the inaugural WNBA teams would have to have an average attendance at away games that is equal or higher than the newest WNBA teams in 2025. The regression for the independent variables would also have to be a negative number or not statistically significant. This would show me that there was no relationship between attendance and year of joining the league.

Conclusion

In conclusion, my analysis finds partial support for the hypothesis that newer WNBA teams have higher game attendance than inaugural teams. The results suggest that newer teams, particularly the Golden State Valkyries, on average have higher average attendance at both home and away games compared to inaugural teams. However, among inaugural teams, there is less consistent evidence of being in the WNBA for a longer period of time impacts attendance. There is variation based on teams, not a blanket statement that being in the WNBA for longer increases attendance. My analysis seems to prove the opposite, being added to the WNBA more recently to increase game attendance at both away and home games.

Despite these patterns, the findings cannot be interpreted causally due to several limitations and confounding variables. Some confounding variables that influence attendance, include player popularity, social media attention, arena accessibility, and broader growth in interest in women’s basketball. For example, increased visibility of star players and social media coverage in recent years may increase attendance for newer teams compared to older ones. Additionally, the data set does not distinguish between fans supporting home versus away teams, which limits the ability to isolate true fan base effects.

If I had more time and money, I would improve this analysis by adding a variable that looked at how individual players on rosters increase or decrease game attendance. Unlike the NBA the WNBA has a culture of loyalty for individual players in addition to support for a team. This is because there is an all star game where most valued players from different teams play against each other. This can affect game attendance because it is more likely that a fan would watch a great player compared to a mediocre one.
